# ArchMorph inspiration and product direction

Recorded from the founder's account on 10 October 2026.

## Inspiration

I have a dream of building a house for my family and being involved in designing it myself.

For almost a year, I tried turning that dream into a floor plan using pen and paper and later Figma. I could imagine the house in my mind, but I was rarely satisfied with the result. Without architectural training, it was difficult to understand room relationships, circulation, dimensions, setbacks, and whether the design would actually feel comfortable.

The biggest limitation was that a flat drawing could not show me what the finished house would feel like. I wanted to see it from the outside, walk through it from the inside, find problems, and keep improving it without needing years of training just to explore an idea.

When I discovered the WebMCP Challenge, I realized I could build the tool I had been wishing for: an architecture studio where a person and an AI agent work on the same live building. That idea became **ArchMorph**.

ArchMorph is intended for people who want to explore their future home visually before taking the design to a professional architect. It does not replace architects or engineers; it makes the early stage of imagining, testing, and communicating a home much more accessible.

## What this means for the product

**Primary user:** A person exploring a future family home, including someone without architectural training.

**Product promise:** Explore the home you want to build. Arrange rooms, see the house from outside, walk through it, and develop the idea with an AI before discussing it with an architect.

The intended journey is:

1. Start with the land, family needs, and known constraints; keep unknown constraints visible as assumptions.
2. Arrange rooms and connections with understandable dimensions and direct visual editing.
3. Inspect the same design in plan, exterior 3D, and Walk Mode to explore scale and movement.
4. Find problems, understand their practical consequences, and revise the live design with an agent.
5. Preserve alternatives and communicate the concept, assumptions, and unresolved questions to a professional architect.

Build decisions should prioritize an understandable, dependable version of this journey. Architectural detail and WebMCP tools support the homeowner's decisions; advanced settings should appear when they are useful. Human and agent edits must remain visible in the same model, and experimentation must be recoverable.

**Success criterion:** A person without architectural training can create a home concept, explain its arrangement, explore it in 3D and Walk Mode, make a revision with an agent, and communicate what still needs professional review. This is a product goal to verify with users, not a claim of completed usability testing or construction readiness.
